# Limitations And Ethics

## Small Data Limits

Sera V0.5 improves symbolic control, but a compact model trained on a small symbolic corpus is still not expected to learn broad style, harmony, or long-range form reliably. The musicality metrics are proxies, not human judgments. A high proxy score does not guarantee artistic quality.

## Dataset And Attribution

Training data should be tracked by source, license, and transformation metadata. Augmented examples must keep links to the original file and augmentation method. Sera should not present generated music as a faithful imitation of a living artist or copyrighted work without appropriate permissions.

## User Transparency

Experiment logs record generator mode, model task type, fallback reason, and validation reports so users can distinguish rule-based output, model-assisted fragments, and fallback behavior.

## Agentic Score Editing Risks

V0.6 lets an Agent propose direct edits to a user's score. This creates new risks beyond generation: over-editing a user's work, misunderstanding a local instruction, violating preserve constraints, damaging musical structure, and blurring authorship when a human and Agent iteratively co-edit the same passage.

Sera mitigates these risks by requiring the Agent to output local `ScorePatch` JSON instead of full MusicXML, previewing patches before application, preserving reject/undo paths, recording operation history, and scoring prompt alignment. These safeguards do not replace human judgment; they make Agent edits inspectable and reversible.

Style requests also need care. The system should avoid claiming faithful imitation of a living artist or copyrighted work. Future human studies should disclose which edits were generated by the Agent and which were accepted or modified by the user.

## V0.7 Additional Risks

LLM-based local score editing can still misunderstand user intent even when output is schema-constrained. A valid patch may preserve MusicXML syntax while changing musical meaning, style, difficulty, or authorship boundaries in ways that the preview does not fully communicate.

Patch preview is a visual and structural aid, not a substitute for listening. Users may accept a patch that looks small but changes phrase direction, harmonic implication, or playability. Conversely, a patch rejected by automatic heuristics may be musically useful after human review.

Over-automation can weaken the user's creative agency if the Agent repeatedly rewrites local material. V0.7 mitigates this with local target ranges, over-editing penalties, partial apply, reject, and undo/redo, but these controls depend on users understanding the Agent's role.

Style-transfer prompts should avoid over-specific imitation of living composers or protected contemporary styles. Operation history and patch history improve transparency for attribution, co-authorship discussion, and later research audits.

## V0.8 Workbench Risks

Closer-to-notation editing creates a stronger impression that the system is a complete composition tool, even though V0.8 still uses simplified layout, heuristic hit mapping, and fake playback fallback. Users may over-trust visual diffs or playback highlights that do not fully represent performance nuance.

Manual-edit preservation reduces accidental overwrites, but it is not a legal or artistic authorship solution. Operation history helps show which edits came from the user and which came from the Agent, yet co-authorship, copyright, and style-boundary decisions still require human policy and disclosure.

Autosave and project packages store detailed creative process data. Future user studies should disclose what is logged, where it is saved, and how it will be used. Researchers should avoid collecting unnecessary prompt, operation, or authorship metadata.
